## 4. Decisions I made for you — reverse any of these

### 4.1 Carrying products forward does NOT copy prices

You said carry-overs are repriced per season per product. So instead of filling the
gap automatically, the UI **shows** it: a "No price" badge per row, a count in the
confirmation, and a filter. The hole is the signal.

*To reverse:* call `runCarryPrices` from the carry-over action. It would need a
colorway filter — it currently takes season, vendor and core only (one line in
`carry-prices.ts`).

### 4.2 Eligibility is derived from `Brand.isLivid`, not stored per product

Loom eligibility is a fact about what a product *is*, not an intent someone ticks.
I keyed it off `Brand.isLivid` and deliberately **not** off `Source`: a Livid
garment is Livid whether it came via Threadflow or Cin7 (87 Loom publications are
Cin7-sourced), and `Source` becomes pure history once Cin7 retires.

Verified safe before relying on it: `isLivid` is set on exactly one brand, and
**no colorway has a null `brandId`** — nothing falls through.

*To reverse:* the rule is three small functions in `readiness.ts`. Change them and
every caller follows.

### 4.3 I did not touch `CatalogGrid`

I planned to make Collections and Publishing reuse it, then dropped that. It is
1,810 lines with editing, paste, copy-down and autosave interwoven; adding a
read-only mode under a clock risks the one surface you said works. Collections got
the same *capabilities* (search, filters, sticky header, full width) in its own
table instead.

*This is the main thing I'd want your call on* — see §5.

## 4a. Open — Loom stock sync contradicts the eligibility rule I just shipped

**This needs your answer before I build anything for it.**

Earlier today: *"external products and vintage should never go to Loom."* I enforced
that — a brand guard on the push, the builder's Loom checkbox withdrawn for external
brands, the Fix grid narrowed to Livid (`0f6ba33`).

Now: *"all platforms must have the same products in their respective databases"* for
stock-movement sync. That requires externals and vintage to **exist in Loom**. Both
statements cannot hold as written.

The likely reconciliation is that Loom plays two roles and eligibility should only
gate one of them:

| Role | Which products | Gate |
|---|---|---|
| Wholesale catalogue (B2B selling) | Livid production only | `isLoomEligible` — as shipped |
| Stock registry (movement sync) | **everything**, so SKUs reconcile | eligibility bypassed |

If that is right, the shape is a **push mode**, and the groundwork exists:
`LoomMode` is already `"full" | "data"`, where `data` means *"a targeted update that
should not be read as the whole picture"* — close to what a registry push is. A
third mode, or a flag on `data`, would bypass both eligibility and readiness (a
stock-sync row needs identity and SKUs, not customs blocks or descriptions) while
`full` stays Livid-only and fully gated.

**What I need from you:** is it "never wholesaled but present for stock", or has the
rule changed? And does Loom actually need prices and attributes for a stock-sync
row, or only identity + SKUs? The second answer decides how much of the payload the
new mode carries.

### The "Archive" / "Storage" season — a real modelling question, not a detail

You suggested carrying stock-sync products into an `ARCHIVE`/`STORAGE` season rather
than FW26. That is the right instinct — it keeps them out of the season a merchandiser
reads as "what we are selling now" — but the `kind` matters, and both options have a
consequence:

- **`kind = REGULAR`** — `classify` parses season codes for `SS/FW`, so `ARCHIVE`
  yields `null` and is skipped (safe). But Collections would show it as a season
  bucket beside FW26 and SS27, which is misleading.
- **`kind = CONTINUITY`** — Collections buckets by `kind`, not code
  (`inContinuity`, `collections.ts`), so `ARCHIVE` would be folded into
  "Continuity (legacy)" alongside the 2,740 Cin7 rows. Same trap the vintage doc
  hit.

Neither is right as-is. My recommendation: a third `SeasonKind` (`OPERATIONAL`, say)
for pools that exist for machinery rather than merchandising — `ARCHIVE` now, and it
is where a `VINTAGE` pool belongs too. Collections then gets a bucket per operational
pool instead of silently merging them into the legacy one. That is a migration, so I
have not done it.

Also note `carry-over.ts` currently **refuses** non-REGULAR targets by design
(`resolveSeason` throws). Carrying into `ARCHIVE` means relaxing that for
operational pools specifically — not removing the guard, which exists to stop
someone carrying products into the legacy Continuity pool by accident.

## 4b. Pagination for Continuity — I would not paginate

You asked for pagination to speed up Continuity. I would push back, for one specific
reason: **pagination is the 500-cap with a UI on it.** A paginated list whose search
only covers the current page is exactly the bug that hid Kai Japan Shadow, dressed
up as a feature.

The load is ~1.2 MB / ~4.5s for 2,740 rows, and rendering is already virtualized, so
the cost is transfer and the server-side query, not the DOM.

Two honest options:

1. **Server-side search, filter and paging.** `getCollections` currently loads every
   colorway and filters in memory, so this is a real restructure of that query — but
   it is the only version where paging and search are both correct. Continuity then
   loads a page in well under a second, and searching hits the database rather than
   the page.
2. **Trim the payload instead.** Much of the 1.2 MB is `tags` (needed only for the
   tag filter) and long names. Sending tag *options* separately from tag
   *membership*, and dropping fields the list does not render, would likely halve it
   for an hour's work rather than a day's.

I would do (2) now and (1) when the list grows past this. Your call — I stopped
rather than guess, because (1) is the bigger commitment and you may find 4.5s
acceptable once you know the alternative.
